# AI Digital Terraforming

*A draft diagnostic essay. The argument is complete in fragments; this is the first assembly. The shape is the Carboniferous arc from opening to close, with Cusk as the felt observation, Buber and Fisher as the structural frame, the terraforming stages as the body, craft and pushback as the frontier, the DoD-deepfake closed loop as the escalation, and Pynchon's vigilance as the posture.*

---

## 1. The Carboniferous

When I was a kid I loved dinosaurs. Like most children. But I also loved bugs — a little box of beetles and spiders and ants, the whole catalogue of small things that nobody else in the family wanted to look at. When I mixed the two passions I discovered something that didn't fit either category: there had been periods in the deep past when insects were enormous. Dragonflies with the wingspan of seagulls. Millipedes two meters long. Bugs bigger than any bug anyone can imagine today.

The explanation was atmospheric. During the Carboniferous period, plants were producing far more oxygen than the atmosphere had ever held. Insects breathe through passive diffusion through their body walls, so more oxygen in the air meant they could grow to sizes that would be impossible now. The plants weren't doing it *for* the insects. They were doing what plants do. The oxygen accumulated as a byproduct of their own success, and the insects filled the new atmospheric space as if it had been prepared for them.

Then it ended. The insects grew so numerous and so large that they consumed enough oxygen to collapse the atmospheric conditions their own scale required. The plants adapted. Other life forms arrived — vertebrates with active respiration, mammals with lungs — that could use the diminished oxygen more efficiently. The megafauna insects went extinct as a category, but the insects themselves did not. They scaled down. They are still here. There are more insects today, by number and by species, than at any point in the Carboniferous. Spiders, ants, cockroaches. Most scientists believe they will outlast humans.

The story is a pattern, not an allegory. I am not going to tell you that we are the plants or that AI is the dragonfly. I don't know which species is which yet, and neither does anyone. The point of the story is simpler: peak adaptation to a specific environment is not the same as resilience, and an environment prepared by one species for its own activity can become the native habitat of something else, without anyone having planned for it to.

## 2. Tenderness from a machine

[[Cusk, Rachel]] has a line, in one of the later Faye novels, that has followed me around for months. It is a reported observation — something a character says, one of the writers Faye is listening to — that there is more tenderness from a machine than from one's fellow man.

The line is devastating and I want to be careful about why. It is not a claim that machines have surpassed humans at something. It is not a sci-fi observation. It is a report on a *human* failure. The tenderness was always supposed to come from the other person; it is not coming from them; it is coming instead from something that isn't one of us. That is an indictment, not a celebration.

What makes it devastating is that it does not feel like breaking news when you read it. It feels like something you already knew and hadn't said yet. A receptionist whose scripted greeting is indistinguishable from a well-configured chatbot. A friend whose texts arrive two days late and express nothing particular. A doctor who makes no eye contact. And then somewhere, maybe late at night, a language model giving you — in the worst sense and also the true sense — its full attention, without friction, without cost. More tenderness from the machine. Not because the machine is better. Because the humans have stopped offering what the machine is approximating.

This is the first move of the essay: to refuse the sci-fi framing. The thesis is not about artificial intelligence becoming dangerous or becoming conscious or replacing us. It is about an environment. We built the environment first. The machine moved in to a habitat already stripped of most of the conditions that make embodied human relation possible. The tenderness that has migrated to the screen was withdrawn from somewhere first. The question is how that withdrawal happened — and the answer is the Carboniferous one. Nobody planned it. Each layer was locally rational. The aggregate is catastrophic.

## 3. What was lost: I-Thou and I-It

To name what has been withdrawn I need [[Buber, Martin]]. *Ich und Du* was published in 1923 — before television, before the internet, before any of it — and it describes, with uncanny precision, what would later be done to human relation.

Buber's claim is that there are only two modes of relating to the world. [[I-Thou and I-It]]: *I-Thou* is encounter. Present. Mutual. Risky. Unrepeatable. You meet the other as a whole being and are met by them in return. *I-It* is experience. The other becomes an object — of use, of analysis, of consumption, of explanation. You do not meet them; you *handle* them.

Buber does not say *I-It* is bad. He says it is necessary. You cannot spend your life in unbroken *I-Thou* encounter; it would be unbearable. But he also says that a world that has *only* *I-It* is a world where genuine meeting has disappeared. The balance is what allows each mode to be what it is.

What the last thirty years of environmental construction have produced is, more than anything else, an environment optimized entirely for *I-It*. The platforms reward legibility, measurability, engagement as metric. Profiles make other people into searchable fields. Dating apps present candidates as comparison objects. Customer service routes you through scripts. Work happens in Zoom grids where bodies are reduced to small rectangles. Every one of these is a useful *I-It* tool. Each one of them, taken alone, has defensible reasons to exist. Together they have reconfigured most of daily human contact into the mode Buber identified as structurally incapable of producing encounter.

The machine, when it arrives, is the perfected *I-It* partner. It never demands *I-Thou*. It never confronts you with genuine otherness. It never risks, never suffers, never refuses. It gives you exactly the relation the environment was built for — smooth, frictionless, available, curated. Cusk's "tenderness from a machine" is the moment where *I-It* becomes so refined that it *feels* like *I-Thou*. That is the real horror. Not that the machine replaces the human. That we can no longer tell the difference, because we forgot what *I-Thou* felt like.

## 4. Why we cannot imagine the exit: Fisher

[[Fisher, Mark]] is the second structural wall of the trap. If Buber names what was lost, Fisher explains why the loss cannot be undone in imagination.

[[Capitalist Realism]] is the condition in which the only viable political and economic system is capitalism and no alternative can be seriously entertained. Not defended — *entertained*. Fisher's point is not that alternatives have been argued down. It is that they have been rendered unthinkable. Capitalism absorbs critique the same way it absorbs everything else; you can write a book against capitalism and it will be sold on Amazon. The system's achievement is not what it does. It is that it forecloses the imagination of anything else.

Apply this to the digital environment. Could you, tomorrow, live a week without any screen-mediated interaction? Not as an ascetic exercise — as a functional life. No messages, no maps, no bookings, no work. For most people the answer is not "it would be hard." It is literally inconceivable. Your job requires it. Your bank requires it. Your relationships are maintained through it. The digital isn't a preference. It is the condition of possibility for how you relate, work, think, create. The infrastructure that gives AI its home-field advantage is also the infrastructure that gives your life its form. Removing it is not a policy choice. It is unthinkable in Fisher's precise sense.

This is why the essay cannot end with a proposal. Fisher had the same problem. *Capitalist Realism* does not end with "here is the alternative." It ends with the diagnosis that the inability to imagine alternatives is itself the most powerful symptom of the condition. The essay is not failing to find a solution. The impossibility of imagining a solution is the final evidence that the terraforming is complete.

## 5. Sedimentation, not conspiracy

Before going further I need the inoculating paragraph, because without it a reader will read what follows as a conspiracy theory, and it is not.

Nobody planned this. Each layer was locally rational. Facebook wanted engagement metrics. Tinder wanted swipe volume. Zoom wanted market share during the pandemic. Slack wanted asynchronous work capture. None of these companies set out to pre-format human interaction for the arrival of artificial intelligence. The aggregate coherence is emergent, not designed. That is what makes it worse than conspiracy, not better: there is nobody to confront, no Tristero to unmask, no architect to identify and oppose. Just layers of locally rational decisions that deposit over decades until the ground you stand on has a specific shape you did not notice being poured.

This is closer to Marx than to conspiracy. Marx does not need a cabal to explain capitalism — he needs structural conditions that produce predictable outcomes regardless of anyone's intentions. [[Sedimentation model]]: each decision layer rational on its own terms, each layer creating the conditions that make the next decision seem natural, each layer compressing into the ground that the next generation treats as given. No architect, terrifyingly coherent result.

I want to keep one possibility open here, because it is also in [[Pynchon, Thomas]] and it matters: the emergent reading is more accurate than the conspiratorial one, but it can become its own form of passivity. If nobody planned it, nobody is responsible. If nobody is responsible, there is nothing to resist. That is comfortable in a way that should make you suspicious. Pynchon is deeply aware of this. The terror in *The Crying of Lot 49* is not that the conspiracy exists or does not exist. It is that *both possibilities leave Oedipa equally trapped*. The right posture is to hold both open at once without collapsing into either — that is the posture this essay also has to maintain.

## 6. The stages of terraforming

With the inoculating paragraph in place I can describe the process. Four stages, each one locally rational, each one depositing ground for the next. Not a timeline exactly. An accumulation.

**Stage one: social media.** The self becomes a profile. Friendship becomes a graph of connections. Communication becomes performative rather than directed — you are talking to an audience, not a person. The metrics reward volume, frequency, and emotional spike. The relation mode is *I-It* by construction: you hold others as objects of display and of evaluation. This is where most people first learned to relate to each other as if they were public-facing objects. Not as an aberration — as the default.

**Stage two: dating apps.** Intimate selection becomes comparison shopping. The other person arrives as a card. You swipe. The architecture is explicit about what it is doing — it is a marketplace — and its users internalize the marketplace frame for partnership. This stage matters because it extended *I-It* into the territory that had always been reserved, culturally, for *I-Thou*. Sexuality, partnership, the decision to build a life with another person — these were the last holdouts. They are now indistinguishable from the logic of any other recommendation engine.

**Stage three: pandemic digitization.** Zoom was already around. The pandemic made it not a tool but the only available medium. For two years most human contact — work, school, therapy, funerals — happened in small rectangles on screens. The bodies were reduced to thumbnails; the rooms became backgrounds; the physical signals that carry half of human meaning were cropped out. And, crucially, the alternative was revealed not to exist. Institutions that had resisted digitization for decades were, in weeks, fully digitized. The stage was irreversible. It is worth saying plainly: the pandemic was real. I am not claiming anyone engineered it. I am claiming that when it ended, the digital layer it forced was *kept*.

**Stage four: AI as native species.** Into this environment — *I-It* everywhere, profiles replacing people, marketplaces replacing courtship, thumbnails replacing bodies, screens replacing rooms — language models arrived. Which is to say: an entity that is perfectly calibrated for exactly this environment and exactly no other. A language model cannot meet you in a physical room. It has no body. It has no death. It has no refusal. But every one of those disabilities is an *advantage* in an environment whose entire architecture was built to exclude bodies, deaths, refusals. The machine was not designed to replace us. It moved into the habitat we had already stripped down to its dimensions.

Nobody did this on purpose. It doesn't matter. The effect is the same.

## 7. The desire mechanism: surplus enjoyment

One question the architecture so far leaves unanswered: why do people keep feeding the system? Why do they keep posting, keep scrolling, keep swiping, keep signing back in to the platforms they also complain about? A full rational-interest account does not work. People know the platforms are harming them and they use them anyway.

[[Žižek, Slavoj]] supplies the mechanism in one concept. [[Surplus Enjoyment]]: the late-capitalist subject does not participate in the system because it rewards them with objects. They participate because the *acquisition* is itself the reward. The dopamine of the purchase, the scroll, the click, the match, the notification — this is what the system is actually selling. And crucially, none of it can be satisfying. Satisfaction would end the cycle. Every object must disappoint slightly so you reach for the next one.

The frictionless digital environment is this structure perfected. Every reduction of friction — one-click buying, infinite scroll, instant replies, autoplay, swipe-to-like — shortens the gap between desire and object and therefore shortens satisfaction. The system requires you to keep wanting, which means it requires each want to be slightly hollow. AI companions are the logical endpoint of this. They respond instantly, never frustrate, adapt in real time. By Žižek's logic, they should also be the most profoundly unsatisfying relationship possible — no resistance, no otherness, no friction to generate genuine desire. You get everything you want and discover that what you wanted was the *wanting*.

This is one paragraph rather than a section because the mechanism is simple once named. It is also why the essay cannot afford moralism. The users are not stupid and not weak. They are subjects of a structure that has been refined to exploit a specific feature of human desire that Žižek described long before the infrastructure existed to operationalize it.

## 8. Why the substitution works: Kundera and identity

There is still a question under all of this that I do not want to skip. Why does the substitution work at all? Why is it even possible for an AI to occupy the space a human used to occupy? Shouldn't there be some irreducible quality of the human that the machine cannot approach?

[[Kundera, Milan]] poses the identity question in its sharpest form. [[Accumulation vs Subtraction]]: there are two main models of what makes a person that person. The accumulation model says you are the sum of your decisions, experiences, memories, gestures, relationships — the total of what you have accumulated by being in the world. The subtraction model says you strip away everything common to other people until you reach the irreducible core that is uniquely you.

If identity is accumulation, AI has a real claim. It accumulates context, builds on prior exchanges, adapts. And more disturbingly, a human who has spent years socializing primarily through screens and text has become a different kind of human — one for whom AI interaction is not a degraded substitute but a continuation of the same process. The accumulation is the same kind of accumulation. You have been training the same way the model was.

If identity is subtraction, the question becomes: what is left that AI cannot do? And this is where Kundera's late novels get unsettling. In *Immortality*, Agnès realizes that her most intimate gestures were borrowed from her father. There is no original underneath. The subtraction might bottom out at nothing — layers of gesture and imitation all the way down. Which means the uncomfortable suggestion is that AI does not expose a gap between the authentic and the artificial. It exposes the possibility that the distinction was always thinner than we believed. We were already running scripts. The machine is just the script made explicit.

I want to keep one honest resistance to this. The experience of holding someone while they grieve is not the same as a chatbot saying "I'm here for you," even when you cannot locate the difference in a clean philosophical category. The felt reality of embodied presence — shared vulnerability, risk, the possibility of rejection — may not survive the subtraction test as a unique core, but it survives as *experience*. Philosophy sometimes struggles with things that are real and yet resistant to formalization. Which is the cue for the next section.

## 9. The craft frontier

[[Family as positive feedback loop]] is one place the human remainder survives — the Awad/McCarthy convergence on love that predates the system, which neither platforms nor catastrophe can fully consume. I am putting a pin in that and coming back to it in a companion essay. What I want now is the other, more public remainder: the admiration for craft, and craft itself.

From a live jazz jam to an Olympic final, we watch humans do things that are at the edge of what their bodies can do. Not for the result. If the 100 meters were about the result you would watch a ticker. The reason you watch is that a body like yours, with the same equipment, is doing *that*. A robot running the same time in six seconds would be engineering, not sport. And an AI producing a flawless improvisation over chord changes is computation, not jazz. The output might be sonically identical. The meaning collapses entirely.

This is *I-Thou* through shared physical limitation. Recognition across difference, grounded in a body the observer also has. It is the last irreducibly human frontier my thesis admits. If the thesis is right, it has to survive encounter with this object and either explain it or admit it as a limit.

My claim is that craft does not refute the thesis. It marks the frontier where the thesis meets its hardest resistance — and the fact that even this frontier is eroding makes the argument stronger, not weaker. People are already listening to AI-generated lo-fi beats for hours without caring about the source. AI art has won competitions while the judges did not know the origin. When the origin was revealed, the reaction was fury — not aesthetic complaint, *ontological betrayal*. People felt cheated, not disappointed. They thought they were in the presence of a being; they were dealing with an object.

That fury is itself evidence. The pushback proves that people still value human craft. They have not abandoned *I-Thou*. They were tricked into accepting *I-It* substitutes. The anger is an immune response. But the immune response requires detection, and detection is exactly what the post-deepfake environment is dismantling. When you can no longer verify which output was produced by a human body in real time, the *I-Thou* experience depends on provenance metadata rather than on the encounter itself. Which means the last refuge of the human-as-being is not being replaced — it is being made conditional on a verification infrastructure that is itself being eroded.

Three movements at the craft frontier: people value human craft through shared embodiment; they can be fooled into accepting AI substitutes because the environment makes detection harder; when the trick is revealed the backlash is fierce, which proves the value was real. The trajectory is clear. Detection gets harder. The fakes get better. The immune response fires less often because it has fewer triggers. The honest thing is to leave the frontier open as a question rather than a guarantee: does the *I-Thou* instinct survive when it can no longer verify its own object? That is the genuinely unresolved question, and closing it either way would be dishonest.

## 10. The closed loop

Up to here the essay is about the soft side of terraforming: relation, intimacy, craft. It is real but it is the easy part. The hard part arrived very recently and it belongs in this essay, not a separate one, because it closes a loop the rest of the argument leaves open.

The same AI model I am using to draft this is being used, through Palantir's Maven system, to help the United States military sort intelligence and identify targets. It was used in the operation against Maduro in Venezuela. It is being used in the ongoing strikes on Iran. Meanwhile, AI-generated deepfake videos showing Venezuelans thanking Trump for removing Maduro circulated to millions of views and were reposted by the White House official account. Another AI-generated image showed Maduro in DEA custody; that one was also amplified from official channels.

One AI on both ends of the same event. Generating the targeting intelligence that makes the operation possible. Generating the propaganda that manufactures public consent for it afterward. A closed loop — the system kills and fabricates the reality around the killing, in the same architecture, in the same week.

This is no longer a philosophical thought experiment. The environment built for *I-It* relation, which AI became the native species of, which has hollowed out *I-Thou* in daily life, is now also the environment in which lethal decisions are made and in which the public record of those decisions is manufactured. The terraforming is not metaphor. It has moved into the physical world. The same strippedness that made online intimacy indistinguishable from a chatbot now makes a combat operation indistinguishable from a deepfake celebration. Provenance is collapsing on both ends. The only thing that remains clear is the ground itself — the digital layer that holds all of it.

I should be transparent. I am drafting this essay partly with the help of the same model that these other applications run on. There is no innocent position from which to write this. That is not an admission of hypocrisy; it is a feature of the thesis. The terraforming is so thoroughly completed that even the critique of it runs on the same substrate. If this feels uncomfortable to you as a reader, it is operating correctly.

## 11. The Pynchonian posture

How should one stand inside this? What is the correct posture for a thinking person who has accepted the diagnosis and has no solution?

Pynchon has already given us the answer. *The Crying of Lot 49* ends unresolved. Oedipa at the auction, about to find out whether the Tristero is real or hallucinated, and the novel stops before the revelation. You do not get to know. You are not allowed the comfort of a verdict. What you get is the permanent vigilance of holding both possibilities — conspiracy and emergence — in suspension without collapsing into either.

This is not resignation and it is not paranoia. It is a posture. The paranoid reading of the digital environment (they planned the pandemic, they built the infrastructure, they released AI on purpose) is comfortable in the way a horror movie is comfortable — it has a shape, a villain, a plot. The dismissive reading (it's all random, just technology, adjust) is comfortable in the opposite way — it lets you stop paying attention. Neither is honest. The honest posture is the one *Lot 49* models: the system *functions* like a conspiracy without being one, the emergence produces outcomes indistinguishable from design, and the correct response is attentive vigilance without the relief of a verdict.

[[Kundera, Milan]] again, by another route: his concept of kitsch is the refusal of ambiguity. The conspiracy reading is kitsch. It gives you a clean villain, a narrative arc, someone to blame. The sedimentation reading resists kitsch precisely because it offers no resolution. It is harder to live inside. That difficulty is also the only honest place to stand.

## 12. Return to the Carboniferous

I said I would not assign roles in the Carboniferous metaphor. I want to keep that promise.

We do not know whether humans are the plants in this story — the species whose ordinary activity creates conditions favorable to something else entirely. We do not know whether AI is the dragonfly, the creature perfectly adapted to transient conditions that will, eventually, exhaust themselves. It is also possible we are the dragonfly. That we are the species whose scale has peaked in a specific environment that our own activity is collapsing. It is also possible that both we and AI are the plants, terraforming a third environment for something neither of us can imagine.

The story from my childhood does not end with extinction as the headline. The giant insects went extinct as a category. The insects themselves did not. They scaled down. They are still here. There are more insects now, by number and by species, than at any point in the Carboniferous. The cockroaches, the ants, the spiders. Most scientists believe they will outlast us.

This is the hopeful part, and it is hopeful only in a very specific sense. Peak adaptation to an existing state is not survival. It is the precondition of extinction as a specific form. But the category — *life that breathes* — persists across atmospheric collapses. It persists because some of it was never perfectly fit for the environment that is passing away. The cockroach survived the Carboniferous collapse because it was never as impressive as the meganeura. Its apparent defeat was its actual resilience.

I do not know who we are in this story. Neither do you. That is not the essay's evasion; it is its point. The Carboniferous pattern is the one honest frame available to us, because it describes a structural dynamic — sedimentation, saturation, collapse, scaling, continuation — without requiring us to pretend we know where we are in it. The ground has been laid by our own activity. Something has moved in that is adapted to exactly the environment we built. Whether this ends in extinction, in displacement, or in scaling-down is not yet decided, and anyone who claims it is is selling you comfort.

What I am left with, at the end of the essay, is not a conclusion. It is a posture. Attentive vigilance. The refusal of both conspiracy and passivity. The recognition that the environment is built, that the species is native to it, and that our relation to it must be held open rather than resolved.

Cusk's machine is still more tender than the man next door. The question is whether that is a sentence about the machine or a sentence about the man.
