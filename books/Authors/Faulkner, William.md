# William Faulkner

## One-line frame
Faulkner renders the American South as Yoknapatawpha — a fictional county where race, class, memory, and the weight of history compress into sentences that refuse to resolve. The prose method is the method: time collapses because consciousness does; the reader is inside the fracture, not looking at it.

## Parallels
- [[Pynchon, Thomas]] — Benjy ↔ Oedipa. Both create disorienting prose but from opposite causes. Benjy: too little cognitive filter, time collapses. Pynchon: too many signals, meaning overloads. Same disorientation, opposite cause.
- [[Wallace, David Foster]] — Nastasya ↔ Caddy. DFW spotted the structural parallel: both are absent centers — women who function as black holes of male consciousness. Men orbit, obsess, try to save or possess. The woman remains opaque.
- Hemingway — prose kinship through the American 20th century; opposite strategies (Hemingway strips, Faulkner accumulates).
- Steinbeck — American landscape and social weight.
- [[Krasznahorkai, László]] — sequential monologues. Baron Wenckheim's theater of voices as Sound-and-the-Fury structure at Hungarian scale.
- [[McCarthy, Cormac]] — Southern lineage continued into apocalypse.
- [[Morrison, Toni]] — the haunted South worked from inside Black American experience rather than from the white Southern interior.

## In tension with
- Readers who require chronological clarity. The Benjy section in *The Sound and the Fury* is famously the hardest — many readers read it twice, once before and once after the other sections, because its time structure only makes sense retroactively. The difficulty is method, not flaw.

## Form / style
Stream of consciousness, fractured time, multiple narrators per novel, no quotation marks in some sections. The prose performs the condition it describes: a family consciousness unable to step outside its own history. Read in English — translation flattens the dialect variation that carries much of the meaning.

## Open threads
- Whether *Absalom, Absalom!* or *Light in August* is the right next step. *Absalom* is more formally ambitious; *Light* is more accessible.
- The degree to which the South-as-Yoknapatawpha method survives being read outside American English — how much of Faulkner is the specific linguistic texture and how much is structural?
