# William Faulkner

## One-line frame
Faulkner treats the American South as an impossibility machine: a place where the past does not pass, trauma has no outside, and consciousness itself becomes the structure of captivity.

## Works read
- [[The Sound and the Fury]] (1929)

- Rest of the Yoknapatawpha cycle — no fixed order yet

## Dialectical partners
- [[Cusk, Rachel]] — structural inverse. Cusk uses absence as escape; Faulkner treats escape as unavailable. Faye vanishes from her own life and keeps functioning. The Compsons cannot vanish and cannot function. Same diagnosis (the self collapses under family weight), opposite prescriptions.

## Parallels
- [[McCarthy, Cormac]] — another American Southerner writing impossible exits. *The Road* works through what Faulkner implies: once the outside also collapses, the family bond is the only remaining structure.

## In tension with
- [[Pynchon, Thomas]] — Pynchon's paranoid architecture keeps the question of external authorship live (is there a system? is it just emergence?). Faulkner forecloses that earlier: no author is needed, the feedback loop runs itself.

## Central concepts
- [[Family as positive feedback loop]] — surfaces across Faulkner, McCarthy, Morrison. In Faulkner the family is the trap that also preserves: you can't escape it and can't live without it.

## Form / style
Stream of consciousness as structure, not technique. The Benjy section is not a representation of confusion — it *is* confusion, navigable only through sensory-trigger association (smell → Caddy → memory → present, in any order, no index). The four voices of *The Sound and the Fury* are not four perspectives on one event; they are four ontologies of the same family.

## Notes
- Vintage 2025 "Complete and Definitive" edition includes the 1945 Compson Appendix, which Faulkner wrote 17 years later to clarify the family. Useful after a first read; spoils the work if consulted during.
