# qntm

*Layer 2 — satellite*

**There Is No Antimemetic Division** relocates cosmic horror from scale to epistemics. The antimeme is an idea or entity shaped so that cognition cannot retain it: the threat is not malevolent, it is that the universe is structured such that certain truths slide off the human mind. The dread is not "something wants you" but "you are built wrong to perceive what is there."

The book grew directly out of qntm's [[Lovecraft, H.P.]]-descended work on the SCP Foundation wiki — the Antimemetics Division series, SCP-3125 — reworked into a fix-up novel. The seams show. The early chapters read as premise-delivery; the Marion Wheeler throughline and the recursive back half are where the real writing lives. Idea canonical, execution competent rather than beautiful — the inverse of the Layer 1 novelists, and the reason this stays a satellite.

The genuinely radical move is underused. Treating *perceptibility* as a property of the world's structure rather than the observer's mind would collapse the perception/reality distinction into a spatial one — but the book mostly defaults to the ontological-but-inaccessible position (it all exists on our plane, we simply can't hold it, so functionally it doesn't). It only brushes true epistemic causation in the final part, when an idea sent into a non-material dimension reaches back to alter reality. The tension is present but not load-bearing, which is precisely why qntm sits in Layer 2: a novelist gesturing at a philosophical idea, not a novel built out of one.

The resolution is formally earned. The machine that is *not not* a machine — an entity that becomes the idea of itself — pays off a book that spent its length training the reader to think in negation-space (antimeme, anti-pattern, the thing defined only by the hole it leaves). A double-negative entity is structurally consistent rather than a twist pulled from nowhere, and the tempo doesn't rush it.

The cleanest contrast in the network is [[Ogawa, Yoko]]: two opposite engines of forgetting. Ogawa's disappearances are imposed by an external authority — slow, bureaucratic, mourned. qntm's antimemes have no author and need not be hostile; they are simply shaped so the loss cannot register *as* loss. Imposed-and-mourned versus structural-and-unmournable.

The nearest neighbor on craft and subject is [[Egan, Greg]] — philosophy of mind through ideas-first SF where the concept outruns the prose, "better ideas than execution" applying to both. The difference is depth of commitment: Egan builds the novel out of the philosophy, qntm brushes it. With [[Liu, Cixin]] the kinship is the concept-forward method and the prose-second cost, but the scaling runs opposite — Liu outward to cosmological despair, qntm inward to the limits of what a mind can hold.
