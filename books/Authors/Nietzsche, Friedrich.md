# Friedrich Nietzsche

## One-line frame
Nietzsche turns philosophy against itself: not "what is true?" but "who needs this truth and why?" The genealogical method treats every value as a symptom before examining it as a claim. The aphoristic form enacts the anti-systematic argument: no single pronouncement is the complete view, because a complete view is itself already a symptom.

## Dialectical partners
- [[Dostoevsky, Fyodor]] — the defining dialectical pair. Not parallel, not tension — *dialogue*. Two writers working the same problem from different positions, each making the other more visible. Shared foundations: comfortable bourgeois morality is a lie; the rational well-adjusted person is either fictional or self-deceiving; suffering is constitutive of being human, not an accident. Divergent prescriptions: Dostoevsky says go through suffering deeper until you hit transcendence (Sonya's grace, Alyosha's kiss). Nietzsche says go through suffering deeper until you transform into something that no longer needs the transcendent (amor fati, the Übermensch). Same descent, different destination — and each destination only makes sense because the other exists as contrast. Nietzsche named Dostoevsky "the only psychologist from whom I had something to learn." The dialogue exists entirely across their works; they never met.

## Parallels
- Kierkegaard — opposite rebellions against Hegel's cathedrals of seriousness. Faith vs. will to power. Masks (Kierkegaard) vs. aphorisms (Nietzsche). Both refuse system.
- Hesse — *Zarathustra* ↔ *Steppenwolf*. In constant dialogue. Harry Haller's crisis is genuinely Nietzschean in that it doesn't resolve into triumph. He doesn't become the Übermensch. He learns to laugh. Possibly the most honest literary response to Nietzsche: the answer isn't power, it's humor.
- Camus — absurd as affirmation. *Myth of Sisyphus* takes amor fati seriously without turning it into a power fantasy. Sisyphus becomes *adequate* to his condition, not superior. Nietzsche without the inflation.
- [[Kundera, Milan]] — eternal recurrence. My reading departs from the conventional take: not repetition giving weight, but iteration giving *quality*. A draft needs revision to become good. Life doesn't get revision. Therefore life is permanently a draft, and the beauty is in accepting the draft condition. Kundera uses eternal recurrence to expose how unbearable it would be — a proper engagement because it takes the idea seriously enough to be disturbed by it rather than empowered.

## In tension with
- [[Han, Byung-Chul]] — will to power inverted. Han argues will to power has turned inward as self-exploitation. The achievement-subject is Nietzsche's Übermensch eating itself.
- Christian moral philosophy — the explicit target of the *Genealogy*.
- Systematic philosophy (Kant, Hegel) — the aphoristic form is itself an anti-Hegelian argument.
- Political appropriations. Every political use of Nietzsche simplifies him; the proper applications are in fiction (Camus, Hesse, Kundera) because fiction can hold the contradiction that political application can't.

## Central concepts
- *Eternal recurrence* — not a cosmology, a test. Would you live this life again, identically, forever?
- *Will to power* — not a drive for domination; a principle of self-overcoming.
- *Master / slave morality* — the genealogical typology.
- *Revaluation of all values* — the method.
- *Amor fati* — love of what is, without qualification.

## Form / style
Aphoristic, polemical, deliberately unsystematic. The fragmented form enacts the anti-systematic claim. Nietzsche cannot be summarized without betraying him; this is structural, not stylistic. The prose is also unusually *funny* — the humor is part of the argument against systems, not a decoration.

## Notes
- Reception history is a minefield. The Nazi appropriation was a falsification his sister engineered, but parts of the text contain material that made appropriation possible. Read the primary, ignore the later Nazi edition, note the tension.
- The Dostoevsky connection is one of the richest moves in modern thought — two writers arriving at the same diagnostic X-ray of modernity from opposite religious/cultural horizons. This is the single most important edge in the 19th-century philosophy node.

## Open threads
- The eternal recurrence — genuine metaphysical claim or pure thought experiment? The text is ambiguous. Commentators split.
- Whether the "revaluation" project is completable or self-undermining. If every value is a symptom, so is the revaluation itself.
- The "Nietzsche is a novelist's philosopher, not a politician's" claim — whether it holds universally or whether a non-reductive political reading is possible.
