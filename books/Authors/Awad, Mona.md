# Mona Awad

## One-line frame
Awad writes fairy-tale horror from inside the body-surveillance system — not satirizing beauty culture from outside but rendering its daily ritual as someone who has lived it. The genre (horror, fairy tale) is a scholar's tool, not a costume.

## Works read
- *Bunny*
- *We Love You Bunny*
- *Rouge*

## Dialectical partners
- [[McCarthy, Cormac]] — family as exit. Same conclusion through opposite methods. *Rouge*: hyper-civilization's trap, exit through mother-daughter love that predates the system. *The Road*: no civilization at all, same irreducible family unit survives. Love as the [[Family as positive feedback loop]] the system can't metabolize. Both locate the structural weakness in a closed system; neither is optimism.

## Parallels
- [[Schweblin, Samanta]] — visceral kinship. Both use the body as the site where the genre operates. Both work from inside women's embodied experience rather than from outside. Awad more baroque; Schweblin more surgical. Shared target; different method.
- Ogawa — memory erasure. *Memory Police*: collective passive erasure, everyone adjusts. *Rouge*: individual active exchange, beauty for memory. Both arrive at Kundera's lightness — a self technically present but emptied of depth.
- [[Han, Byung-Chul]] — beauty as self-exploitation. *Rouge*'s skincare rituals are Han's self-exploitation in practice: voluntary, pleasurable, consuming. Nobody forces the optimization. The subject performs it on herself, for herself, and the system needs no Big Brother.
- [[Fisher, Mark]] — realism in a face mask. *Rouge* is capitalist realism applied to beauty: the system feels natural, the exploitation feels like care, alternatives are unimaginable.
- [[Žižek, Slavoj]] — beauty-truth from inside. Žižek theorizes the beauty-goodness-truth equation as ideological mechanism; *Rouge* shows it from inside, where the character experiences the equation as care rather than exploitation.
- Stoker — blood → beauty exchange. Same Faustian architecture: *Dracula* takes blood / gives immortality; *Rouge* takes memory / gives beauty. The victim wants it. Harker's journal fragmentation = Belle's memory loss. Mirrors as unreliable perception. Gothic horror updated for the beauty industry.
- Goethe — Faustian bargain as deep structure. Trade something essential for something desired. The bargain always costs more than advertised. Goethe formalized the template Awad inherits.
- King — maximalist horror. Both comfortable with genre-forward fiction that expects readers to take it seriously. Different registers (King suburban/domestic American; Awad body/beauty specific) but shared willingness to be unsubtle when the scene calls for it.
- Female Gothic — possible lineage. The lineage from the Gothic through female writers reclaiming body-horror as a tradition that addresses women's embodied experience specifically. Awad is scholar-aware of this lineage (PhD territory).

## In tension with
- García Márquez — surface resemblance. Both use fantastic elements; the resemblance is cosmetic. García Márquez saturates reality with magic ambient-ly; Awad concentrates the magic into a specific transactional bargain. Different project entirely.

## Central concepts
- [[Family as positive feedback loop]] — the structural answer *Rouge* and *The Road* both arrive at. Not just that family saves you passively — the caring generates more capacity to care. Belle's mother sacrificed herself to protect Belle's memory; that sacrifice is what eventually allows Belle to recover herself. Protection generates recovery generates gratitude generates understanding of what was sacrificed. The loop can fail (both books show this), which is what keeps it from being naive.
- *Beauty-for-memory* exchange — *Rouge*'s central bargain, where the cost of beauty is specifically the memory of the mother. Fairy-tale structure (you always pay for magic; the price is always something essential to identity) applied to the specific contemporary horror of beauty culture.
- *Body surveillance from inside* — the distinguishing feature of Awad's method. Not written as critique from outside; the product details feel real because they are.

## Form / style
Maximalist, sensory-saturated, rhythmically propulsive. Long sections of descriptive surrender to product, skin, mirror, color — the excess is the argument, because the system itself operates through excess. Refuses the minimalist temptation that would make the critique more respectable at the cost of losing the texture of the pathology.

Genre-competent: she knows fairy-tale structure, horror pacing, Gothic conventions. The PhD is visible without being professorial.

## Notes
- Biographical: Egyptian Muslim father, French-Canadian Catholic mother of Serbian and Irish descent. Born Montreal, 1978. MFA at Brown, PhD in Creative Writing and English at Denver. Edinburgh master's dissertation on fear in the fairy tale. That last detail is structural: the use of fairy tale in *Bunny* and *Rouge* is scholar-informed, not decorative.
- *13 Ways* is the autobiographical root: the body-surveillance in later novels isn't researched, it's lived. This is what distinguishes her work from beauty-culture satires written from outside the pathology.
- Margaret Atwood named Awad her "literary heir" in the NYT's *T Magazine*. Not a trivial endorsement — it places Awad in the lineage of female writers using genre (horror, speculative, fairy tale) to examine what systems do to women's bodies and consciousness.
