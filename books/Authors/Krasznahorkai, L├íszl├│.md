# László Krasznahorkai

## One-line frame
Krasznahorkai writes the apocalypse as ambient condition rather than event — social decay that has already happened and is still happening, narrated in sentences that refuse to end because ending would imply a shape.

## Works read
- [[Baron Wenckheim's Homecoming]] (2016)

## Works on shelf / to read
- *Sátántangó* — queued after *Gravity's Rainbow*

## Dialectical partners
- [[Sebald, W.G.]] — both work with the sentence as a unit of endurance, both are post-catastrophe writers in a Central European tradition. Sebald is elegiac; Krasznahorkai is bleaker, more farcical, less forgiving.

## Parallels
- [[Kafka, Franz]] — the bureaucratic-nightmare lineage. Krasznahorkai's provincial Hungarian town functions as Kafka's Castle without the mystery — the opacity is not a secret, just the texture of reality.

## In tension with
- Conventional apocalyptic fiction, where the event is dramatic and the subject is response. Krasznahorkai says: the event already happened, no one noticed, and "response" is the wrong category.

## Central concepts
- [[Ordinary decay as apocalypse]] — Baron Wenckheim does not depict a catastrophe; it depicts the permanent condition after one that nobody can date.
- [[Kierkegaardian indirect communication]] — the professor-Baron parallel in Baron Wenckheim reads as a structural claim about where transcendence can actually occur (not in doctrine, only in doubled or displaced figures).

## Form / style
Sentences that run across pages, paragraphs that run across chapters. The prose rhythm is not stylistic excess but a claim about reality: there are no natural stopping points, so the grammar refuses them. English translations by Ottilie Mulzet and George Szirtes preserve this; the effect is closer to reading Bernhard than to reading Sebald.
