# Byung-Chul Han

## One-line frame
Han treats the late-modern subject as a closed system that exploits itself, making external coercion redundant. The diagnostic vocabulary (burnout, transparency, positivity, the end of ritual) names phenomena most political theory still reads as personal rather than structural.

## Works read
- *The Burnout Society*
- *The Transparency Society*
- *The Disappearance of Rituals* / *The End of Ritual*
- *Psychopolitics*
- *The Agony of Eros*

## Dialectical partners
- [[Fisher, Mark]] — parallel diagnoses from different genealogies. Fisher names the system; Han describes the experience of living inside it. Fisher: imagination is closed. Han: the subject has internalized the disciplinary apparatus so completely there's no exterior to imagine from. Reading them together produces a more complete picture than either alone.
- Orwell / Huxley — Han identifies a *third* model of control that makes the Orwell-Huxley binary look incomplete. Orwell: pain and surveillance. Huxley: pleasure and distraction. Han: self-exploitation through achievement. The burnout society doesn't need Big Brother or soma because the subject exhausts themselves voluntarily.

## Parallels
- Heidegger — direct lineage. *Being and Time* as the temporal ground of existence. Han asks: what happens when the ritual temporal structures dissolve? We lose what oriented us. *The Disappearance of Rituals* is Heideggerian temporality applied to the digital present.
- [[Kundera, Milan]] — transparency = kitsch. Kundera's investigation of kitsch (the erasure of ambiguity, the demand for everything to be legible and positive) is essentially what Han formalizes as the transparency imperative. Same flattening of interiority, different political system. The problem isn't the regime — it's something deeper in modernity itself.
- DFW — the exhaustion society, the infinite self-optimization, consciousness trapped in perpetual achievement. *Infinite Jest*'s territory, diagnosed.
- Awad — *Rouge*. Skincare rituals as Han's self-exploitation in practice: voluntary, pleasurable, consuming. Nobody forces the optimization.

## In tension with
- [[Žižek, Slavoj]] — Han argues the neoliberal subject is self-exploiting *without* external ideology; Žižek insists ideology remains operative at the libidinal level. Productive disagreement.
- [[Nietzsche, Friedrich]] — will to power inverted. Han argues will to power has turned inward as self-exploitation. The achievement-subject is Nietzsche's Übermensch eating itself.
- McCarthy — answers Han. Han says the self-exploiting subject is trapped alone. McCarthy (with Awad) answers: the exit is lateral, toward another person, through a bond the system can't reach. Not optimism — locating the structural weakness in a closed system.
- Positive psychology, wellness discourse — Han specifically names these as the pathology, not the cure.

## Central concepts
- [[Burnout Society]] — the core diagnostic. Central to the [[AI Digital Terraforming]] thesis.
- *Transparency society* — the tyranny of visibility, the flattening of interiority.
- *Achievement society* — the subject as entrepreneur of itself.
- *The disappearance of rituals* — the loss of symbolic structure as a specific modern pathology. Connects to Sebald (how we relate to the past when ritual containers for memory dissolve) and to the whole "what do we do when lost" cluster.

## Form / style
Very short books — rarely over 100 pages. Aphoristic chapters. Philosophical, not empirical — Han argues by conceptual analysis and vivid contemporary examples, not by data. This is a feature, not a bug: the books are designed to land a diagnostic frame, not prove it.

## Notes
- The short-book form makes Han's work unusually fast to read but easy to misunderstand as shallow. The concepts are doing heavy philosophical work under the surface; re-reading rewards.
- Writes in German despite being Korean — worth tracking what this bilingualism does to the style.

## Open threads
- Whether Han's diagnosis generalizes beyond the specific post-industrial, hyper-connected context he's writing from.
- Han cycles through short books with overlapping themes — whether the corpus compounds into a single argument or stays a series of framings without synthesis.
