# Albert Camus

## One-line frame
Confronts the absurd through revolt, art, living fully. Sisyphus must be imagined happy — affirmation, not despair. The opposite response from Sartre to the same recognition.

## Works read
- *L'Étranger*
- *La Peste*
- *Le Mythe de Sisyphe*

## In tension with
- Sartre — split on political violence.
- [[Goethe, Johann Wolfgang]] — opposite responses to the absurd. Faust refuses through total knowledge; Sisyphus accepts through labor.

## Parallels
- [[Nietzsche, Friedrich]] — absurd as affirmation. Direct genealogy.
- [[McCarthy, Cormac]] — carrying the fire. Father as Sisyphus.
- [[Murata, Sayaka]] — outlier indifference. Meursault destroyed for it; Keiko adapts.
- Hemingway — flat prose, hidden depth.
- Orwell — political engagement.
- Beckett — absurd as theater. Sisyphus and Godot.
- [[Martí, José]] — engaged rebel.
- Kierkegaard — absurd secularized.
