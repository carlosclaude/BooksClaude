# Mark Fisher

## One-line frame
Fisher names the closure of political imagination as a cultural condition rather than an ideology — and then refuses to let that closure off the hook, even after identifying it. Diagnosis without resignation.

## Works read

## Dialectical partners
- [[Žižek, Slavoj]] — same question, different answer. Fisher says imagination is closed; Žižek says desire is captured by surplus enjoyment. Both account for voluntary participation without needing false consciousness. Fisher is darker — Žižek still believes in rupture; Fisher doubted it. The contradictions Žižek finds dialectically productive, Fisher argues have been neutralized — capitalist realism absorbs critique the way it absorbs everything else.

## Parallels
- [[Han, Byung-Chul]] — converging diagnoses from different genealogies. Fisher names the system; Han describes the experience of living inside it. Fisher asks why we can't imagine alternatives; Han asks what the system does to interiority.
- DFW — one of the strongest connections. Fisher wrote depression as political condition, the privatization of suffering. DFW's entertainment-addiction loop is capitalist realism experienced from inside a single consciousness. Both identify the same mechanism: a system that makes its effects feel like personal failures rather than structural conditions. The footnotes in DFW are the individual struggling to think clearly inside Fisher's atmosphere.
- [[Kundera, Milan]] — capitalist realism as kitsch. Kundera's kitsch operates through ideology (communist or capitalist); Fisher's operates through the elimination of the capacity to conceive alternatives. Kundera's subjects at least know something is being denied; Fisher's have forgotten that denial is occurring.

## In tension with
- Classical false-consciousness accounts. Fisher specifically argues people often *know* the system is broken; knowing doesn't change behavior. This breaks with most 20th-century critical theory.
- McCarthy — interesting tension. *The Road* literally writes what Fisher says is easier to imagine than the end of capitalism. McCarthy can write the end of the world. Nobody in the network writes the end of the system.
- Orwell / Huxley — Fisher explicitly engages both and argues we got neither. Not Orwell's pain or Huxley's pleasure but something more insidious: a bland, depressive acceptance that this is all there is.

## Central concepts
- [[Capitalist Realism]] — the master concept. The widespread sense that capitalism is the only viable system, paired with the inability to imagine alternatives. Central to the [[AI Digital Terraforming]] thesis.
- *Hauntology* (via Derrida) — the lost futures that haunt the present.
- *The slow cancellation of the future* — culture recycling itself, unable to imagine forward. Maps onto the digital environment's flattening of temporal experience into an eternal present of feeds.
- *Acid communism* — the late, unfinished counter-current.

## Form / style
The *k-punk* blog posts are the purest Fisher: short, fast, embedded in specific cultural objects (albums, films, TV), working outward to the structural claim. *Capitalist Realism* the book compresses this into 80 pages that do not waste a sentence. The k-punk method is what the [[AI Digital Terraforming]] essay is doing — thinking in real time, testing ideas against daily life, without resolving into clean academic argument.

## Notes
- All three *k-punk* volumes read. The collected form is essential — the posts accrete into an argument the individual pieces don't make alone.
- Fisher's suicide in 2017 is not external to the work. The depression he diagnosed politically was also his own condition. The theorist of depression as political condition succumbing to it is part of the node, not biographical trivia.
