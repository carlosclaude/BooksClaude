# Capitalist Realism

## Definition
The widespread sense that capitalism is the only viable political and economic system, paired with the inability to even imagine a coherent alternative. Not an ideology defended in argument — a condition in which the question of alternatives has become structurally unthinkable.

## Origin
Mark Fisher, *Capitalist Realism: Is There No Alternative?* (2009). Fisher extends a remark widely attributed to Jameson and Žižek — "it is easier to imagine the end of the world than the end of capitalism" — into a sustained diagnosis. Fisher's later work (the *k-punk* volumes) elaborates the cultural-texture consequences: aesthetic exhaustion, the impossibility of the new, depression as political symptom.

## Mechanism
Operates less by suppressing dissent than by pre-empting it. Alternatives are not argued against; they are rendered unserious in advance. The culture recycles its own surface (nostalgia, pastiche, reboots) because the future has been closed off as a genuine category. Individual psychological symptoms (anxiety, depression, burnout) get interpreted as personal pathology rather than as effects of the system, which reinforces the system's invisibility.

## Why it matters
It's the concept that explains why pointing out that a system is working badly does not, by itself, produce political change. The diagnosis must include the closure of imaginative alternatives, not just the empirical critique.

For the [[AI Digital Terraforming]] thesis: capitalist realism is the structural reason the digital environment sedimented the way it did. Each local decision (use the platform, post the content, optimize the self) was rational given that the alternative — non-participation — had already been rendered unthinkable. No architect, no conspiracy: just locally rational behavior inside a closed imaginative horizon.

## Related concepts
### Genealogical
- Jameson's "postmodernism as the cultural logic of late capitalism" — Fisher's direct predecessor
- Gramscian hegemony — the older term for the kind of consent-without-coercion Fisher describes

### Parallel
- [[Surplus enjoyment]] (Žižek) — different mechanism (libidinal), same question (why do people voluntarily participate in what exploits them?)
- [[Burnout society]] (Han) — the psychological phenomenology of living inside capitalist realism

### In tension with
- Frankfurt School false-consciousness accounts — Fisher specifically argues that capitalist realism is not mystification in the classical sense; people often know. Knowing does not change behavior.

## Counter-concepts
- Prefigurative politics / genuine utopian imagination — what capitalist realism specifically rules out
- "Acid communism" — Fisher's late, unfinished attempt to name a counter-current

## Open questions
- Whether the concept has become self-confirming — if everything is legible as capitalist realism, the concept explains nothing specific.
- Whether there's a post-Fisher reformulation that accounts for the current moment (AI, platform consolidation) or whether the framework needs extension.
