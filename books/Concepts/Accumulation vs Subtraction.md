# Accumulation vs Subtraction

## Definition
Two rival models of personal identity. *Accumulation*: a person is the sum of what they have done, thought, chosen, experienced, remembered, and been in relation to — identity as total deposit. *Subtraction*: a person is what remains when everything common to others has been stripped away — identity as irreducible core, the uniquely *this-person* that survives the elimination of the generic.

The pair frames a single structural question: is identity constructed additively from experience, or found subtractively beneath experience?

## Origin
[[Kundera, Milan]] poses both models across *The Unbearable Lightness of Being* and, more directly, *Immortality*. The paired formulation as the choice between accumulation and subtraction is my naming — the vault treats it as a concept because the two models produce sharply different answers to the question of whether AI substitution is even possible.

Kundera's own endpoint is unsettling for both. In *Immortality*, Agnès realizes that her most intimate gestures were borrowed from her father. There is no original underneath. The subtraction might bottom out at nothing — layers of gesture and imitation all the way down.

## Mechanism
The two models generate different answers to the question *what makes a person that person*:

- **Accumulation mechanism.** The self is constituted by decisions, memories, relationships, writings, photographs, online activity. More accumulation = more self. Loss of accumulation (amnesia, erasure, dementia) is loss of self. The self has no essence before the accumulating begins.
- **Subtraction mechanism.** The self is what remains when you strip away cultural inheritance, learned behavior, borrowed gestures. There is a residue, a core, a uniquely individual remainder. The self has an essence that the accumulation merely dresses.

The models have radically different implications for the question of substitution. If accumulation is correct and the accumulation is what matters, then anything that can accumulate equivalent context *is* equivalent as a self. If subtraction is correct and there is a unique residue underneath, then no imitation no matter how perfect reaches the residue.

## Why it matters
For the [[AI Digital Terraforming]] thesis, this is the frame that answers the most disturbing question the essay raises: *why does AI substitution even work?*

If identity is accumulation, AI has a real claim to something like personhood — or at least to performing personhood convincingly. It accumulates context. It builds on prior exchanges. It adapts. And more disturbingly: a human who has spent years socializing primarily through screens and text has become a different kind of human — one for whom AI interaction is not a degraded substitute but a *continuation* of the same process. The accumulation is the same kind of accumulation. You have been training the same way the model was.

If identity is subtraction, the question inverts. What is left that AI *cannot* do? And this is where Kundera's late-novel answer ("the subtraction might bottom out at nothing") becomes unsettling in a specific way: AI does not expose a gap between the authentic and the artificial. It exposes the possibility that the distinction was always thinner than believed. We were already running scripts, already borrowing gestures, already layers of imitation. The machine is just the script made explicit.

Neither model, taken alone, provides the reassurance humanism wants to provide. That is exactly why the concept is load-bearing for the essay.

One honest resistance, which belongs in this note and in the essay: the experience of holding someone while they grieve is not the same as a chatbot saying "I'm here for you," even when the difference cannot be located in a clean philosophical category. The felt reality of embodied presence — shared vulnerability, risk, the possibility of rejection — may not survive the subtraction test as a *unique core*, but it survives as *experience*. Philosophy sometimes struggles with things that are real and yet resistant to formalization.

## Illustrated by
### Works
- *The Unbearable Lightness of Being* ([[Kundera, Milan]]) — the weight/lightness frame in which the accumulation/subtraction question first surfaces for me
- *Immortality* ([[Kundera, Milan]]) — Agnès and the borrowed gestures; the subtraction model tested to its breaking point
- *The Memory Police* ([[Ogawa, Yoko]]) — accumulation model tested through forced subtraction; each disappearance removes weight until existence can barely sustain itself
- *Rouge* ([[Awad, Mona]]) — the beauty-for-memory exchange as a choice to trade accumulation for surface; the horror is discovering too late that the subtraction bottom-ed out

### Authors
- [[Kundera, Milan]] — the source
- [[Ogawa, Yoko]] and [[Awad, Mona]] — opposite-direction illustrations of what subtraction feels like as narrative experience
- Pirandello (not yet a full note) — *One, No One and One Hundred Thousand*; 1926 prophecy of the selfhood crisis, useful external check

## Related concepts
### Genealogical
- Locke's memory-theory of personal identity — ancestor of the accumulation model
- Cartesian *cogito* and its descendants — ancestors of the subtraction model (the irreducible thinking self)
- Nietzsche's genealogy of values — a kind of anti-essentialism compatible with the subtraction-bottoms-out-at-nothing reading

### Parallel
- [[Surplus Enjoyment]] ([[Žižek, Slavoj]]) — different domain (libidinal), parallel question (what constitutes the subject's continuity?)
- [[Capitalist Realism]] ([[Fisher, Mark]]) — the cultural condition in which accumulation has become the default identity model almost without contest, because the platforms assume it

### In tension with
- Strong essentialist accounts of selfhood that insist on a pre-experiential core
- Pure narrative-identity theories (MacIntyre, Ricoeur) that treat accumulation as itself the answer without worrying that the accumulation might be borrowed all the way down

## Counter-concepts
- The *gesture theory of identity* — Kundera's own late move; the self is constituted by gestures that were always borrowed. Not a third model but a radicalization of subtraction (what's underneath is: more borrowing)
- Embodied presence as uncategorizable remainder — the honest resistance described above; the phenomenon survives both tests without resolving into either

## Open questions
- Whether the dichotomy is genuine or whether a third model is available. Candidates: relational identity (Buber, Levinas), narrative identity (Ricoeur), embodied identity (Merleau-Ponty). Each can be read as a specific collapse of the two poles rather than a genuine third term.
- How the accumulation model fares once the accumulation happens partly through AI. If your writing, your reading, your texting, your decisions are increasingly co-produced with language models, whose accumulation is it?
- Whether the "subtraction bottoms out at nothing" finding is specifically modern (post-Nietzsche, post-Kundera) or always true and only now being articulated. This matters for how stable the thesis's diagnosis is.
